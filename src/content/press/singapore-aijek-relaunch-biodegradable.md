Singapore designer **Danelle Woo** is bringing **Aijek** back after a multi-year hiatus, with **Straits Times** coverage placing the relaunch around **24 September 2026**. Public reporting describes a tight collection built exclusively on biodegradable, plant-based natural fabrics — and an explicit refusal of even small polyester blends — after personal material experiments that left synthetics in the soil long after cotton, linen and similar fibres had returned.

Lace, once a signature, reportedly gave way because so much commercial lace is polyester-led. In its place: **kerawang**, a traditional Balinese cutwork embroidery practice, worked with original lotus motifs across a compact collection. Production is described as concentrated with a small circle of Bali factories the designer knows personally, with sales online and by appointment — a deliberate downshift from earlier high-growth ambition.

For QuintaEarth’s Europe ↔ SEA lens, Aijek’s second act is a maker parable: sustainability as *restraint engineered into cloth and labour relationships*, not as a CSR annex. Luxury here is proximity — knowing the workshop, choosing fibres that can complete a life cycle — while staying honest that “biodegradable” is still a claim that EmpCo-era scrutiny will want scoped and evidenced when goods face EU consumers.
