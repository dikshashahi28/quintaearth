In February 2026, trade titles including **MediaNews4U** and **Campaign India** reported that Gurugram-based **Confiance Communications** won the India public-relations mandate for **The Ocean Cleanup**, with a brief to shape national and regional narratives around the organisation’s India work — technology, deployments, and civic context — rather than parachuting a global slogan unchanged.

The maker-adjacent lesson sits beside the hardware. Interceptors and river systems only become public fact when journalists, municipalities, and residents receive accurate, localised explanation. Purpose-led PR done well is not spin; it is translation of engineering into civic language — monsoon calendars, urban waste infrastructure, trust.

For greentech operators entering India or SEA megacities, budget the narrative layer with the same seriousness as maintenance crews. Avoid metric inflation. Cite partners you actually have. Let the machine’s results speak when they exist; until then, describe process honestly. QuintaEarth is not on this mandate — we note the public appointment as a signal that ocean cleantech now competes on credibility craft as well as capture rates.
