## The problem

Most car trips in European cities are short. Many are made by parents carrying children or shopping, the very trips that ordinary bikes handle badly. A car for these jobs is cheap per trip once it is owned, but it takes street space, costs money to keep and emits CO2 and pollutants on exactly the short, cold-engine journeys where emissions are worst.

The Dutch had a low-tech answer for decades: the bakfiets, a heavy bike with a wooden box in front. It worked on flat Amsterdam streets but was hard work on hills, over long distances or with three children aboard. Electric assistance changed that, and Urban Arrow set out to redesign the whole bike around the motor rather than bolt one on.

## The product

The **Urban Arrow Family** is a long front-loading cargo bike with an aluminium frame and a box made of expanded polypropylene (EPP), the same kind of foam used in children's car seats and helmets. Urban Arrow describes the box as acting "like a large inverted helmet". Its published specifications ([Urban Arrow](https://www.urbanarrow.com/family-bikes/family)):

- price from EUR 4,999 in Europe;
- maximum total weight 250 kg, with a maximum rider weight of 125 kg;
- room for up to three children with three-point seatbelts, or a baby car seat with an adapter;
- electric pedal assist up to 25 km/h, with an average range of about 50 km per charge;
- 274 cm long, 70 cm wide, 110 cm tall and 50 kg.

The bike uses a Bosch mid-drive motor and an Enviolo stepless hub, so riders do not need to change gear. In August 2026 Urban Arrow presented the **FamilyNext²**, with a redesigned rear frame, integrated cables, an enclosed chain case, tool-free seat adjustment and a phone mount, plus a new rear rack that can take a fourth child ([Bicycling.nl](https://www.bicycling.nl/nieuws/urban-arrow-vernieuwt-bakfiets-met-introductie-van-de-familynext)).

## How it works

1. **Load:** children sit on a bench in the foam box, belted in; shopping goes at their feet or in the box alone.
2. **Ride:** the rider pedals as on any bike, and the motor adds power up to 25 km/h.
3. **Steer:** a linkage turns the front wheel ahead of the box, with a low centre of gravity for stability.
4. **Shift:** the stepless hub adjusts the gear ratio smoothly, even when stopped at traffic lights.
5. **Protect:** a rain cover turns the box into a cabin in bad weather.
6. **Charge:** the removable battery charges from an ordinary socket at home or work.

## Impact and numbers

- **Company scale:** Kreek told the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung in 2023 that Urban Arrow would sell its 100,000th vehicle that year and that Germany would overtake the Netherlands as its largest market by 2024 or 2025 ([Merkur](https://www.merkur.de/wirtschaft/lastenradhersteller-urban-arrow-deutschland-2025-groessten-markt-machen-92567470.html)).
- **Market:** Germany's bicycle industry association counted 220,300 cargo bikes sold in 2025, 185,300 of them electric, with e-cargo bikes making up 9.5% of all e-bike sales ([cargobike.jetzt](https://www.cargobike.jetzt/ziv-marktdaten-2025/)). In Switzerland, e-cargo bike sales rose 244% between 2019 and 2024, although they are still only 3.4% of e-bike sales there ([SVI summary](https://www.svi.ch/media/upload/publications_de/02cac22b_1a_3_Dimitri_Marcinek_Cargo_bike_OUVEMA_DM_250908.pdf)).
- **Car replacement:** in a Swiss survey of 696 cargo bike owners, 25% said they had given up a car after buying a cargo bike and a further 6% had given up a second car or were doing so; 38% had reduced car use, 21% were already car-free and only 10% reported no change ([Marincek et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11116-024-10569-3); [SVI summary](https://www.svi.ch/media/upload/publications_de/02cac22b_1a_3_Dimitri_Marcinek_Cargo_bike_OUVEMA_DM_250908.pdf)).

**Honest caveats.** The Swiss survey covered owners of all brands, not Urban Arrow riders specifically, and people who buy cargo bikes are often already keen on cutting car use, so the effect on a typical family may be smaller. Urban Arrow publishes no life-cycle carbon figure for the Family, and a 50 kg bike with a motor and battery has a bigger footprint than a normal bicycle, although far smaller than a car. At EUR 4,999 or more it is a premium purchase: about a third of the Swiss owners had received a subsidy. Parking a 2.7 metre bike is hard in many flats and old streets, and theft is a real risk, which is why Urban Arrow now sells a dedicated battery lock. Safety matters too: on 1 October 2026 Gazelle USA recalled about 1,150 Urban Arrow bikes sold between 2016 and 2022 because the front seatpost bolt can break; no injuries were reported ([Bicycle Retailer](https://www.bicycleretailer.com/recalls/2026/10/01/gazelle-usa-recalls-some-urban-arrow-cargo-e-bikes-because-faulty-seatpost-bolt)). The 100,000 figure is a co-founder's statement, not an audited number.

## What's next

Growth now depends less on the bike and more on cities: protected bike lanes wide enough for cargo bikes, secure parking at homes and stations, and purchase subsidies. Urban Arrow also sells Cargo and Tender models for business deliveries, where the same logic applies to vans in city centres. Within Pon, which also owns Gazelle and Cervélo, it can draw on a large dealer and service network ([Bicycle Retailer](https://www.bicycleretailer.com/international/2019/11/19/pon-bike-acquires-urban-arrow)).

## Why it matters for Europe / green buyers

The European Declaration on Cycling, signed by the Parliament, Council and Commission in 2024, commits the EU to more and safer cycling infrastructure, support for a European cycling industry and better data ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/C/2024/2377/oj/eng)). Cargo e-bikes are where cycling meets car replacement: for families, a cargo bike can be the difference between owning one car and two. Cities and employers that offer subsidies, leasing or cargo bike sharing make that switch easier.

India already moves goods and children on pedal tricycles, cycle rickshaws and scooters, but premium European cargo bikes are priced far beyond most households. The lesson for Indian cities is not the product but the system: safe lanes, parking and simple, locally made electric cargo cycles that compete with a scooter on cost.
